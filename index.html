About

Building reliable robot manipulation from models, sensing, and real-time software.

I am a robotics software engineer with applied R&D experience across the full path from task-level commands and mathematical models to reliable motion on physical robots. I design kinematics and dynamics algorithms, deterministic motion and control software, sensor and hardware interfaces, and validation workflows using C++, ROS 2, Pinocchio, LabVIEW RT and FPGA, MATLAB and Simulink, and Python.

At Lab INIT Robots, I develop a real-time ROS 2 and C++ whole-body control system for a 10-DoF mobile manipulator with a Kinova Gen3 arm. The 400 Hz pipeline coordinates Cartesian motion, arm and base control, redundancy resolution, trajectory execution, teleoperation, joint-limit handling, and safety-oriented pose and velocity commands on physical hardware.

I have also exercised whole-body control in Gazebo and MuJoCo, completed PyTorch reinforcement-learning experiments in simulation, and adapted contact-data interfaces between simulators. These simulation results are presented separately from my physical robot deployments.

My current development direction is Learning from Demonstration for manipulation. It builds on completed work in teleoperation, IMU, EMG, depth-camera motion acquisition, cross-simulator contact modeling, and model-based execution to develop data-collection and policy-execution pipelines.

1. Analytical Redundancy Resolution for the 7-DoF Kinova Gen3

A swivel-angle formulation selects a smooth elbow configuration while preserving the commanded end-effector pose.

My contribution

Simulation

Stack

Learning-Based Robust Predictive Control for a 7-DoF Rehabilitation Exoskeleton

Gaussian Process modeling, constrained MPC, and robust compensation support responsive trajectory tracking on the physical exoskeleton.

Technical problem
Track constrained exoskeleton motion despite nonlinear dynamics, model error, and human-robot interaction forces.
My contribution
Integrated a Gaussian Process residual model with constrained MPC and robust sliding-mode compensation.
Hardware
Physical 7-DoF ETS-MARSE rehabilitation exoskeleton.
Stack
Gaussian Processes, model predictive control, C++, and LabVIEW RT.
Measured result
Approximately 387 μs per control iteration on the physical system.
